~/nexus /README.md

Why this exists

Nexus exists to bring first year Computer Science students at Nelson Mandela University up to the level the rest of the world is already working at, starting with the modules sitting on your desk right now.

And the uncomfortable part nobody in academia wants to say out loud: most people do not have the attention span to learn from a textbook. Courses are still built on the assumption that they do, and that assumption is exactly why capable students fall behind.

  • 01The notes we get are sufficient. They really are. But sufficient is not the same as understanding, so I took them apart and rebuilt them with clearer structure and worked examples.
  • 02AI fluency is quietly becoming a requirement that universities have not yet decided how to qualify. Learning these tools now, while you are still studying, is the difference between catching up and leading.
  • 03Everything is aimed at one outcome: making you genuinely competitive with students anywhere in the world, at a price a student can actually afford.
  • 04For some of us learning just clicks. For the rest it takes hearing the same idea a different way, in our own words, at our own pace. That is what the study partner is for.

A quick word about me: I am a first year CS student at NMU building this in my own time because I remember how disorienting that first semester is. If something here is wrong or unclear, tell me and I will fix it.

~/nexus /partner

It learns you back

Not a search box returning a definition. A study partner that adapts to how you actually understand things.

student why does the chain rule work? i just dont get it
partner It comes from two things: the chain rule itself, and the fact that y is a function of x. Because y depends on x, and x depends on u, the rate of change multiplies. You already know this from velocity and acceleration: if v changes with time, acceleration is the derivative of v with respect to time. Same shape of idea.
student ok but show me with numbers
partner Let x = u² and y = 3x. Then dy/du = dy/dx × dx/du = 3 × 2u = 6u. At u = 2: dy/du = 12.
