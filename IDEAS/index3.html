IWhy this exists

The notes we get are sufficient. Sufficient is not the same as understanding.

The problem Courses are built on the assumption that everyone can sit with a textbook for six hours. Most people cannot.

Nexus exists to bring first year Computer Science students at Nelson Mandela University up to the level the rest of the world is already working at, starting with the modules sitting on your desk right now. The notes you get on campus are genuinely good. I have read them closely and I have nothing against them. What they do not do is show you why.

What I changed Clearer structure, worked examples, and the reasoning behind every step instead of the steps alone.

So I took them apart and rebuilt them. Every derivation gets the reasoning. Every formula arrives somewhere before it is used. If you have ever closed a textbook chapter and realised you could follow the symbols but not the argument, that gap is the one I am trying to close.

The part nobody mentions AI fluency is becoming an unofficial job requirement.

The industry is not going to wait for universities to decide what to call a qualification in it. Learning to work with these tools now, while you are still studying, is the difference between catching up later and leading from the start. And for the students it does not click for immediately, an AI that learns how you learn is the difference between giving up and getting it.

Learning just clicks for some of us. For the rest, it takes hearing the same idea a different way, in our own words, at our own pace.

A quick word about me, because you should know who is writing this: I am a first year Computer Science student at NMU, building this in my own time because I remember exactly how disorienting that first semester was. I am not a company. I am one person who figured some of this out the hard way, and would rather hand you the shortcut than watch you struggle through it. If the notes help, wonderful. If something is unclear or wrong, tell me and I will fix it.

IIIThe study partner

An AI that learns you back

Coming with the notes. Not a search box that hands back a definition.

  • It adapts to how you actually understand

    Some people learn from a diagram. Others need the same idea repeated in different words. The partner reads which explanation is landing and adjusts without being asked, so you are never stuck re-reading the same paragraph hoping it clicks this time.

  • It knows the module it is teaching

    Scoped to your first year subjects and grounded in these notes, so it explains MATT101 the way your lecturer means it, rather than the way the internet tends to.

  • It never gets tired of the question

    Ask the same thing nine times and it will answer nine times, differently if it has to. Which is the patience you actually need at two in the morning, three weeks before an exam.
